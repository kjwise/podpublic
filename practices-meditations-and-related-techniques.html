Part X

Practices, Meditations, and Related Techniques

Estimated reading time: 15 min

Find a practice by what it helps you do. Most entries are retrieval notes, not complete instructions; Solo Orbit is given in full. Use the relevant chapter for the full method and Checklists and Materials for preparation and screening.

Safety & Contraindications

If overwhelm, dissociation, panic, or compulsive drive appears, stop and return to grounding.

ABORT PROTOCOL: Stop the practice. If no emergency sign is present and you remain oriented, return to ordinary breathing, orient to the room and your feet, and contact support. Call emergency services for chest pain, severe breathlessness, fainting, seizure, loss of consciousness, severe confusion or agitation, dangerous overheating, or inability to remain safe. Do not re-enter until stable and the cause has been addressed.

Everyday Attention and Grounding

Awareness & Regulation

For the fuller body-based sequences, see Chapter 31: The Embodied Anchor. Keep eyes open when that supports orientation. Use sound or contact points when visual imagery is unhelpful, and adapt duration to energy and capacity.

  • Body Scan: Move attention through the body, noticing sensation without forcing change. Skip areas that overwhelm; a partial scan is enough.
  • Boundary grounding: Exhale long twice; orient; trace one hand’s outline; name a boundary (“Not now”); take one small protective action.
  • Grounding Exercises: Return attention to physical support and surroundings. Chapter 31 gives Rooting Cord and Sensory 5-4-3-2-1. Use concrete sensation if imagery is unhelpful; sit or lie down if standing is unsafe.
  • Meditation / Mindfulness: Train attention and return by noticing thoughts, feelings, and sensations without immediately reacting. Presence Cultivation and Stillness Practice name related ways of returning to present contact.
  • Mindful Breathing: Notice the natural rhythm without manipulating it.
  • Orienting: Look or listen around the room and notice present surroundings.
  • Situational Awareness: When a message thread makes it hard to choose a reply, pause and return to the actual request. Chapter 13’s “The Digital Threshold” gives the practice.
  • Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR): Gently tense and release muscle groups to notice the difference between effort and release.
  • Self-Soothing Touch: Rest a hand on the heart, stroke an arm, or hug yourself when touch feels comforting.
  • Shielding Practice: Exhale; orient; imagine a breathable edge at the skin or arm’s length; set one rule. If anxiety rises, drop the image and return to Orienting.
  • Somatic Awareness: Track bodily sensation and the “felt sense” without pressing for an explanation.
  • Somatic Triad: Exhale → Orient → Sensation. Stay with one sensation until it steadies. See Chapter 1: Awakening the Dragon.

Comfortable Breathing

Keep breathing comfortable. Stop patterned breathing for dizziness, tingling, breath hunger, or agitation and return to ordinary breathing.

  • Alternate Nostril Breathing (Nadi Shodhana): Alternate gently between nostrils to establish a steady rhythm. Chapter 12 uses it alongside movement and reflective writing.
  • Comfortable breathing with a longer exhale: Lengthen the spine, soften the front, and let several breaths move evenly without holds. Allow the exhale to become comfortably longer.
  • Box Breathing: Equal counts for inhale, hold, exhale, hold, such as 4-4-4-4 seconds. Skip holds if uncomfortable.
  • Coherent Breathing: A slow, regular rhythm, often around 5–6 breaths per minute; comfort governs the pace.
  • Breath reflection on exchange: Notice shared air, gravity, and contact through several unforced breaths. Inspired by Chapter 6.
  • Breath as Interface: Chapter 13’s three-breath reset: arrive, release the previous mood, choose one quality to carry forward.
  • Diaphragmatic / Belly Breathing: Let lower ribs and abdomen move easily; do not aim for a larger breath.
  • Soft Belly Breathing: Allow the belly to soften with the inhale.

Movement & Embodiment

  • Mindful Eating: Attend to taste, texture, smell, and the experience of eating or drinking.
  • Movement / Embodiment Practices: Yoga, Tai Chi, dance, or other accessible movement for sensation, expression, strength, and presence. Chapter 31 includes movement and contained exertion.
  • Self-Massage: Use your hands or simple tools for comfortable touch and pressure.

Reflection & Contemplation

  • Journaling / Specific Journaling Prompts: Write to clarify feeling, track patterns, or explore one question. Chapter 31 offers sensation-led prompts.
  • Nature Connection / Forest Bathing (Shinrin-Yoku): Attend to a natural place through the senses.
  • Micro-Útiseta: A brief sensory vigil outside at night or at an open window, while grounding remains intact. Listen to distant sounds or the texture of city noise without labelling. Choose a physically safe location and dress for weather. See Chapter 40: The Sage’s Compass.
  • Evening Recollection: Give one unfinished matter attention when useful, then put it aside. An uneventful day needs no review. See Chapter 13.
  • Self-Reflection / Self-Inquiry: Examine a thought, motive, belief, value, or recurring behaviour honestly.
  • Five Energetic Bodies scan: Notice what registers in Form, Eros, Soul, Archetypal, and Void experience. Chapter 3 introduces the map.

Daily Integration

  • Daily Touchstones: Small anchors at waking, thresholds, conflict, and night. The full sequence opens Part VIII (The Unfolding Path).
  • Alchemical Vessel: Sacred Space, Sacred Time, Sacred Record: one place, a repeatable window, and a brief note of state, action, and consequence. See Chapter 4.
  • Fractal Resonance — The Tuning Fork: Chapter 44’s short state-to-trait reset. If numb, flooded, or blended, ground first. When witnessing is available, lengthen the spine, let the exhale outlast the inhale, recall one core value, stay for three breaths, then take the next small step. See “Fractal Resonance — The Tuning Fork” in Chapter 44: The Resonant Dragon.

Emotional and Relational Work

Use these when you can remain oriented, choose, and stop. When relational or trauma material exceeds that capacity, work with experienced, grounded support.

Breath and Sensation

  • Gentle Settling Practices: Comfortable breathing, humming, or chanting to settle. Keep breath unforced; stop for dizziness, breath hunger, or agitation. Cold exposure is a separate stressor below.
  • Breath-Sensation Link: Attend gently to one area of tension through several unforced breaths. If focusing there activates you, return to ordinary breath. See Chapter 31.
  • Conscious / Somatic Breathwork: Intentional breath patterns for working with state and emotion. For rapid, forced, or prolonged methods, see Intense Breathwork & Altered States below.
  • Ocean Breath (Ujjayi Pranayama): A soft, audible narrowing at the back of the throat during inhale and exhale, used for steady attention.

Emotional & Shadow Work

For complex trauma, significant dissociation, suicidality, self-harm risk, severe shame, or high-risk trauma material, use an appropriately trained licensed mental-health clinician rather than self-study. This applies to parts dialogue, imagery, grief, and Shadow Work below.

  • Active Imagination: Jungian engagement with spontaneously arising imagery, dialogue, movement, or creative material.
  • Dialogue with Self / Shadow / Archetypes: Write or speak with an inner part to hear its perspective without treating it as an instruction.
  • Grief Work / Tears as Release: Make room for loss, anger, sadness, and crying. Stop solo work if orientation or safety weakens. Chapter 20 develops grief practice.
  • Hoʻoponopono: Traditional Native Hawaiian communal reconciliation belongs to its lineage and context. Chapter 19 offers a distinct Dragon-inspired inner-reconciliation adaptation, not the traditional practice.
  • Tracing a Trigger: Follow sensation → story → choice. See Chapter 15’s “Tracing a Trigger.”
  • Wielding the Flame: A solo ritual for releasing a belief, pattern, or identity. See Chapter 15’s “Wielding the Flame.”
  • Ritual of Reclamation: Welcome exiled parts through self-consent. Any witnessed version requires explicit agreement and pacing. See Chapter 15’s “Ritual of Reclamation.”
  • Parts Work With Resourced Contact: Let present-time safety meet a wounded part without forcing exposure or disclosure. Chapter 19 provides the fuller sequence.
  • Polarity Journaling: Track when Structure becomes rigid or Flow loses contour. Chapter 22 supplies the polarity map.
  • Shadow Work: Bring disowned material into conscious relationship and accountability. Chapter 15: The Shadow Threshold houses the named rituals above.
  • Tree vs. Graph Journaling: Map a situation as a hierarchy, then as a network of relationships. Chapter 10 supplies the distinction.

Somatic & Trauma-Informed Principles

  • Titration: Approach charged sensation, emotion, or memory in small doses.
  • Pendulation: Alternate between a manageable amount of activation and present-time resource. Chapter 31 develops pacing; learn trauma-focused applications within therapeutic support.

Movement, Energy & Embodiment

  • Cold Exposure: A physiological stressor, not a settling technique. Start gradually; consult a qualified clinician for cardiovascular issues. Never practise alone in open water or combine cold exposure with breath-holding.
  • Energy Work: Subtle-energy practices vary by tradition. Seek instruction that distinguishes its own maps from medical treatment.
  • Qi Gong: Chinese practices coordinating posture, movement, breath, and attention; learn the chosen form with qualified instruction.
  • Sound Healing / Mantra / Chanting: Use voice, instruments, or repeated phrases to explore sound and state.

Relational & Ethical Practices

Chapter 34: Tools for the Path gives the main relational tools; Chapter 33 develops facilitation.

  • Accountability Practices: Name conduct and impact, make welcome amends, and change the pattern. Repair does not require renewed contact.
  • Active Listening: Hear and reflect what is said without taking over, fixing, or defending.
  • Community Agreements / Group Norms: Agree how to speak, pause, disagree, repair, and opt out before charge rises.
  • Lineage Respect & Humility Practices: Learn the context of a borrowed practice and remain open to correction.
  • Feedback Mechanisms / Peer Supervision: Provide accessible participant feedback and seek challenge from peers or mentors. Internal consultation is not independent review.
  • Interpersonal Integrity: Notice what you bring and what arises between people. See Chapter 13, “Contact in the Field.”
  • Metta (Loving-Kindness): Polishing Indra’s Net: Widen goodwill from oneself towards others, using Chapter 6’s jewel-by-jewel image. Goodwill does not authorize unwanted contact, help, or touch.
  • Nonviolent Communication (NVC): Marshall Rosenberg’s sequence of Observation, Feeling, Need, and Request; Chapter 34 gives an embodied application.
  • Setting Boundaries: Name limits and availability, then establish the agreements and actions that protect them.
  • Social Media Ethics Check: Review accuracy, impact, consent to sharing, and responsibility before posting.
  • Trust Inventory Exercise: Locate which thread is fraying: sincerity, reliability, competence, or care.
  • Wheel of Consent Practices: Betty Martin’s map distinguishes Serving, Taking, Accepting, and Allowing by who acts and whom the action is for.

Creative & Contemplative Practices

  • Contemplation: Stay with one theme, question, paradox, or symbol.
  • Creative Expression: Give feeling or experience form through art, music, writing, or dance.
  • Dreamwork: Record and explore dream imagery; disturbing or trauma-linked material may need trained support.
  • Systems mapping / Systems Thinking: Diagram connections, feedback, and influences. Chapter 10 develops the network lens.
  • Play & Reconnection: Make room for spontaneous, non-goal-oriented enjoyment. Chapter 19 returns to play.
  • Ritual: Use symbolic action to mark a transition, loss, intention, or commitment. The named Shadow rituals above provide examples.
  • Visualization: Explore possibilities or rehearse action through imagery. For traumatic material, use the clinical-support boundary above.
  • A brief Void moment is a lower-intensity pause without deliberate loosening of identity boundaries. It is not the Dragon’s Plunge; stop and ground if orientation or agency shifts. See Part VII (Void Meditation).

Mindset & Approach

  • Art of Not-Knowing / Sitting with Uncertainty: Stay with ambiguity without forcing closure.
  • Focused Immersion / Deep Work: Protect bounded time and attention for a subject or practice.
  • Harm Reduction: Reduce foreseeable harm while preserving informed choice.
  • Lived Paradox / Holding Paradox: Let conflicting truths coexist without forcing a resolution. Chapter 13 offers “Lived Paradox.”
  • Working with limits: Use a real constraint to focus attention or creativity. See Chapter 9.
  • Embracing Change & Impermanence / Releasing Attachment to Outcome: Loosen rigid expectations without becoming indifferent. Chapter 7 explores this distinction.
  • Trusting the Process / Trusting the Self-Organizing Process: Tend sleep, food, movement, or one honest conversation before forcing a story. See Chapter 7.
  • Embracing Emergence: Notice what is ending and beginning, then make one small deliberate choice. See Chapter 7.

Breathwork, Altered States, and Erotic Practice

Use the Threshold Safety Checklist and method-specific screening in Checklists and Materials before entering. Preparation, support, and aftercare must include the ability to return to ordinary orientation and choice.

Intense Breathwork & Altered States

Rapid, forced, or prolonged breathwork: Review the Rapid, Forced, or Prolonged Breathwork Screen in Checklists and Materials. Relevant conditions require clinician review; a clinician’s “no” is binding. These exclusions do not apply wholesale to ordinary mindful or slow breathing.

  • Holotropic Breathwork — HIGH RISK: Rapid, connected breathing with evocative music. Never self-facilitate or combine with intoxicants.
  • Transformational Breathwork — HIGH RISK: Connected-breath intensives, sometimes with bodywork or sound, that can evoke intense emotion or traumatic material.

Both require verified training and scope, screening, explicit touch consent and stop signals, sober support, emergency response, and planned aftercare. Brand certification is not clinical licensure. Stop for dizziness, chest symptoms, panic, dissociation, or loss of orientation. Overbreathing can produce medically significant symptoms.1

Void Meditation — HIGH RISK: The Dragon’s Plunge can loosen the sense of separate self; dissociation, panic, destabilization, and loss of agency are stop signs. This is not medical treatment. Use the Psychological Stability and Return-Capacity Screen in Checklists and Materials, a grounded witness or anchor, and planned aftercare. Chapter 37: Steps into Infinity — The Dragon’s Plunge gives the complete protocol, distinct from a brief Void moment.

Psychedelic Use — EXTREMELY HIGH RISK: For context, see Chapter 30. Before considering use, review the Substance-Specific and Medication Screen in Checklists and Materials, legal requirements, and the actual support available.

Do not proceed during acute mania or psychosis, suicidal crisis, severe dissociation, impaired consent or orientation, or inability to remain safe. Seek urgent mental-health or crisis support for acute symptoms or safety risk. Pregnancy, breastfeeding, and possible medication interactions require substance-specific clinician review. An unsupported setting remains a no-go.

For nonclinical use, do not redose or add another substance. Arrange sober support, a low-stimulation setting, explicit aftercare, and a clear escalation plan. Facilitated or higher-intensity settings also require explicit consent agreements, accountable medical or mental-health screening, emergency readiness, and phased integration support.

If distress begins: do not redose or add another substance. Move to a low-stimulation setting with sober support. Call emergency services for seizure, collapse, chest pain, loss of consciousness, dangerous overheating, severe agitation or confusion, or inability to remain safe. Poison Control in the United States, or local toxicology support elsewhere, can advise alongside emergency care or for exposures without those signs.2

Shadow, Power & Sexuality Work

Solo Orbit — The Flame Liberator

The Solo Orbit is an inward practice for learning to inhabit erotic charge without rushing towards release, performance, suppression, or relational enactment. The physical dose is deliberately small, but the erotic and symbolic material can intensify; stop and return to grounding whenever choice or orientation narrows.

The aim is to separate arousal from release so charge can become sensation, attention, and creative heat rather than command.

Prepare. Stay sober. Treat up to three gentle cycles as a complete small dose. Empty your bladder. Sit with the spine comfortably long or stand with the knees soft. If you have pelvic pain, known pelvic-floor tightness, or difficulty with urination, bowel movements, or sexual function, skip the contractions and seek guidance from a qualified pelvic-health clinician before practising. Stop for anxiety, numbness, dissociation, pain, bracing, or any sense that you are overriding your limits.

Keep the effort gentle. Do not strain, bear down, or hold your breath.

Generate heat. Bring attention to the perineum and inhale slowly into the belly, allowing the pelvic floor to soften. On an easy exhale, gently contract the pelvic floor as if lifting the base of the pelvis upwards. Picture a red ember growing brighter with the lift.

Release fully before the next inhale. Let the pelvic floor drop or bloom as the belly fills. The release matters more than the contraction. Complete this phase when subtle warmth, tingling, or density begins to gather at the base of the spine. If you feel little, do not add repetitions; reduce the effort and prioritise release.

Let it rise. When charge has gathered, let the next gentle lift accompany an easy exhale and imagine the heat drawing up the spine, vertebra by vertebra, past navel and heart towards throat and crown. Follow the imagined current rather than forcing it. Release before the next inhale. If the chest catches or the charge feels heavy, soften the effort, widen the collarbones, sigh, and let some of the sensation drop before continuing.

Diffuse and return. At the top, keep breathing normally while attention rests there for a moment. Let the red fire diffuse into white light or golden mist, then follow the next easy exhale back down over the skin and through the body into the ground. Move through the sequence without chasing release. Stop before fatigue, bracing, or strain. If the sensation stays subtle, trust subtlety.

Stop and land. If you feel light-headed, drop your gaze, release any lift, return to ordinary breathing, and feel your feet. If sensation becomes jagged, overwhelming, or pools uncomfortably in the pelvis or chest, stop the practice. Place a hand where the charge gathers and let the next easy exhales travel into the legs and feet. Finish with several slow, unforced exhales, orient to the room, drink water if needed, and do something ordinary before returning to your day.

For the Keeper of Eros, archetypal meaning, and ethical frame that govern this practice, see Chapter 21.

For all partnered or shared erotic work below, use Partnered Eros and Kink: Minimum Safety Floor in Checklists and Materials. It governs consent, stop authority, active structural leverage, physical risks, de-roling, and aftercare.

  • Conscious Transgression — EXTREMELY HIGH RISK: Taboo or Shadow work that moves from inward symbolism into shared enactment. Requires cultural and ethical oversight, crisis-response plans, and trauma-informed integration.
  • Kink Practices — HIGH RISK: Negotiated power or archetypal play, without guaranteed therapeutic benefit. Physical risks require qualified, activity-specific education. Do not mix kink with alcohol, psychedelics, dissociatives, or other states that impair consent. A sober facilitator cannot restore an intoxicated participant’s consent capacity.
  • Sacred Sexuality Practices — HIGH RISK: Shared erotic work requires safer-sex measures and facilitation proportionate to the container. Seek qualified somatic-sex educators or therapists for emotional fallout. Chapter 21 gives the inward-facing default.
  • Tantric Practices — HIGH RISK: Advanced mantra, yantra, pranayama, ritual, or sexual practices require lineage-qualified teaching, clear initiation agreements, preparation, aftercare, and community accountability.

Professional Therapeutic Modalities

These are referrals, not self-study instructions. Psychotherapy and medical treatment require licensed professionals acting within scope. For nonclinical methods, verify training, scope, supervision, screening, and emergency response. A method certificate alone does not confer clinical licensure.

  • Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT), Mentalization-Based Therapy (MBT), Schema Therapy, Transference-Focused Psychotherapy (TFP): Use licensed mental-health professionals trained in the method.
  • Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR): Work with a licensed EMDR therapist.
  • Internal Family Systems (IFS) / parts-informed therapy: Apply the complex-trauma and clinical-support boundary under Emotional & Shadow Work.
  • Neurofeedback: Requires a qualified, certified practitioner.
  • Somatic Experiencing (SE™): Psychotherapy or complex-trauma treatment requires an appropriately licensed clinician trained in SE; an SEP credential alone is insufficient.
  • Trauma Release Exercises (TRE®): Learn initial exercises from a trained provider with an explicit nonclinical or licensed clinical scope.
