The Five Gates of Grief

Grief is the solvent that dissolves the armor around the Shadow. Each gate names a kind of sorrow, the protective pattern guarding it, and one practice for meeting it cleanly.

GATE I

Impermanence

Protective Pattern: Fear of endings, clutching, control.

Practice: Meditate on impermanence, write a private letter to what was lost, and make a symbolic release.

GATE II

Wounded Parts

Protective Pattern: Shame, self-rejection, unworthiness.

Practice: Compassionate dialogue with the shamed parts. If trauma is acute, do not carry it alone.

GATE III

Collective Pain

Protective Pattern: Numbness, cynicism, paralysis.

Practice: Tonglen within your Window of Tolerance: breathe in the reality of suffering, breathe out compassion.

GATE IV

Unmet Needs

Protective Pattern: Resentment, lack, the ache of chronic emptiness.

Practice: Name the specific unmet need, acknowledge the pain, and ask how that need can be met now.

GATE V

Lineage

Protective Pattern: Inherited trauma, family secrets, unconscious loyalties.

Practice: Explore family history respectfully. Honor what came before you and acknowledge what still echoes now.

Note on Shame

Shame often guards these gates. Ask: “Who taught me this shame? Is it mine?” Then root again in self-acceptance.

Pillar and Wave

Pillar and Wave picture two capacities of one living body: steadiness that can hold, and responsiveness that can yield. Structure and Flow loosely resonate with yang and yin, but the correspondence is partial. Neither capacity belongs to a sex, gender, or fixed role.

The Pillar

An image of Structure

Feet meet the floor; back ribs fill; a gaze settles. A boundary can remain warm without bracing.

The Wave

An image of Flow

A jaw unclenches; attention widens to sound or touch. Yielding can remain alert without becoming disappearance.
