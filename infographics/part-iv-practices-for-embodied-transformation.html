The Solo Orbit

Ignition Without Extraction

Separate arousal from release so Eros becomes steady, inhabitable, and integrable. Internal by default. Up to three gentle cycles. Slow, safe, and real.

1. The Root Lift

Let the pelvic floor soften on an easy inhale. On the exhale, make one gentle lift, then release fully before the next inhale. Do not strain, bear down, or hold your breath.

2. The Ascent (The Dragon Rises)

Let the next gentle lift accompany an easy exhale as you imagine heat rising through navel, heart, and throat. Release before the next inhale. If the chest catches, soften and sigh.

3. The Crown Expansion

At the crown, let red fire diffuse into white light or golden mist, then exhale it back down over the skin into the earth.

4. The Return

Stop before fatigue or strain. Finish with several slow, unforced exhales, orient to the room, drink water if needed, and return to ordinary grounding.

Stop Signs

Stop for pain, bracing, anxiety, numbness, dissociation, light-headedness, overwhelming or pooled charge, or any sense of override. Skip contractions and seek pelvic-health guidance for pelvic pain, tightness, or urinary, bowel, or sexual-function difficulty.

Micro-Dose

One gentle lift and full release, then a hand on the chest and a quiet line of consent to yourself: “Slow, safe, and real.”

The Five Gates of Grief

Grief is the solvent that dissolves the armor around the Shadow. Each gate names a kind of sorrow, the protective pattern guarding it, and one practice for meeting it cleanly.

GATE I

Impermanence

Protective Pattern: Fear of endings, clutching, control.

Practice: Meditate on impermanence, write a private letter to what was lost, and make a symbolic release.

GATE II

Wounded Parts

Protective Pattern: Shame, self-rejection, unworthiness.

Practice: Compassionate dialogue with the shamed parts. If trauma is acute, do not carry it alone.

GATE III

Collective Pain

Protective Pattern: Numbness, cynicism, paralysis.

Practice: Tonglen within your Window of Tolerance: breathe in the reality of suffering, breathe out compassion.

GATE IV

Unmet Needs

Protective Pattern: Resentment, lack, the ache of chronic emptiness.

Practice: Name the specific unmet need, acknowledge the pain, and ask how that need can be met now.

GATE V

Lineage

Protective Pattern: Inherited trauma, family secrets, unconscious loyalties.

Practice: Explore family history respectfully. Honor what came before you and acknowledge what still echoes now.

Note on Shame

Shame often guards these gates. Ask: “Who taught me this shame? Is it mine?” Then root again in self-acceptance.

Somatic Toggle (Pillar and Wave)

Conflict, Leadership, Creativity, Repair

Pillar rehearses Structure/Yang as internal authority and boundary. Wave rehearses Flow/Yin as receptivity and integration. These are energetic principles, not gender roles.

The Pillar

Structure / Yang

Feet wide, spine erect, gaze steady. Let steadiness arrive without bracing: “I am the container. I hold the space. I am solid.”

Use before a difficult negotiation, a boundary, or any moment you are tempted to collapse or appease.

The Wave

Flow / Yin

Soften belly and jaw. Let the exhale lead. Surrender can stay responsive rather than collapse: “I receive. I allow. I trust the flow.”

Use when rigid, anxious, controlling the uncontrollable, or stuck in leadership stress, creative blocks, or relational gridlock.
